⚛️ Quantum & Dimensions


Quantum theories of consciousness take seriously the idea that quantum mechanics plays a necessary, if not sufficient, role in the specific generation of phenomenal consciousness in certain physical entities like brains—beyond the general application of quantum mechanics in all physical entities. However, these theories suffers of lacks consensus, clear mechanisms, and sustained empirical support; many scientists remain skeptical of quantum–mind connections.


⭐⭐⭐ Wigner–von Neumann’s Consciousness-Collapse: Experience at the Quantum Cut


👾 Von Neumann distinguished two ways a quantum state can change. He formalized two fundamentally different kinds of quantum evolution. His “Process 2” is the deterministic, continuous, reversible evolution of the quantum state according to the Schrödinger wave function equation. Measurement introduces “Process 1,” a nonunitary and probabilistic transition associated with definite measurement alternatives. The two are formally incompatible, and nothing in the theory specifies when the first applies rather than the second. Moreover, the problem is that the measuring apparatus itself consists of quantum matter. If system and apparatus are treated quantum mechanically, their interaction merely entangles them: the superposition moves outward rather than disappearing.


  • Predictions of the theory are unaffected by where along this chain one inserts Process 1—the "cut" is movable, and this is a theorem rather than a convention. But it must be inserted somewhere, since without it no definite outcome ever appears. The chain has one terminus that cannot itself be absorbed into the quantum description: what von Neumann called the observer's "abstract ego," the subjective perception of a result.
  • It was Wigner who turns von Neumann’s measurement problem explicitly into a mind-body problem. Quantum mechanics, he argues, ultimately connects probabilities between successive “impressions” or sensations registered by observers. He defines consciousness remarkably phenomenally—as “the property of having sensations”—and proposes that appropriate physicochemical organization gives rise to consciousness whose richness varies with the complexity of its substrate (Wigner, 1961). Unsurprisingly, Wigner does not begin with a disembodied Cartesian soul: physical organization profoundly determines consciousness and its phenomenal contents.
  • As a theory about phenomenal consciousness, the Wigner proposal has an unusual strength: it assigns subjective experience a fundamental causal role that standard physicalism typically denies. Phenomenal consciousness is not merely what the brain does viewed from inside; the occurrence of a particular conscious experience may alter subsequent physical evolution by selecting—or inducing the selection of—a definite quantum alternative. Mind thereby enters fundamental dynamics.
  • However, the theory does not explain why phenomenal consciousness exists. Wigner explicitly asks how physicochemical conditions give rise to sensations.
  • Another unresolved issue is the threshold. Wigner entertained graded consciousness in animals and even primitive organisms or plants, but offered no principled physical criterion specifying which systems possess enough phenomenal consciousness to alter quantum evolution.
  • If phenomenal consciousness can influence quantum state reduction, quantum processes might in turn participate essentially in the generation or organization of phenomenal consciousness itself.


⭐⭐⭐ Penrose-Hameroff’s Orchestrated Objective Reduction


Penrose-Hameroff's quantum consciousness, which they call Orchestrated Objective Reduction (OrchOR), is the claim that consciousness arises in the fundamental gap between the quantum and classical worlds. Consciousness is non-computational, yet still explained by the physics of neurons, but a physics distinct from and broader than that which we currently understand.

Penrose argues that the missing physics that describes how the quantum world becomes the classical world “is the only place where you could have non-computational activity.” But he admits that it’s “a tall order” to sustain quantum information in the hot, wet brain, because “whenever quantum systems become entangled with the environment, ‘environmental decoherence’ occurs and information is lost.”


  • Consciousness emerges from quantum state collapses in neuronal microtubules, linking brain activity to spacetime geometry.
  • Bridges quantum mechanics and classical physics, making consciousness a product of quantum gravity effects.
  • Faces skepticism over sustaining quantum coherence in the brain and lack of direct experimental confirmation.
  • Orchestrated quantum reductions in microtubules avoid decoherence, enabling non-computational processing and unified conscious moments.
  • Suggests consciousness predates life, explains binding and causation, and potentially accounts for anomalous experiences.


The claim is made that experimental evidence shows that anti-depressants, psychedelics and general anesthetics, which selectively alter or block consciousness, all act via microtubules. Some evidence suggests that entangled states can be maintained in noisy open quantum systems at high temperature and far from thermal equilibrium—for example, counterbalancing decoherence by a “recoherence” mechanism—such that, “under particular circumstances, entanglement may persist even in hot and noisy environments such as the brain."


  • The Orch OR theory proposes that consciousness arises from orchestrated (Orch) quantum state objective reductions (OR) in microtubules within brain neurons, which connect, adherents say, to the fine-scale structure of spacetime geometry. Adherents posit that Orch OR accounts for cognitive binding, real-time conscious causal action (through non-computable Penrose OR and retroactivity), memory encoding, and, ambitiously, the hard problem of phenomenal experience. Moreover, consciousness as a non-local quantum process in spacetime geometry provides potentially plausible mechanism for near-death and out-of-body experiences, pre-cognition, afterlife and reincarnation.


⭐⭐ Hameroff’s Consciousness Came Before Life


"Evolution may have worked to optimize, organize, and prioritize more advanced conscious experience involving memory, belief, forecasting, intention and iteration, driven by primitive, and then more advanced forms of pleasure-seeking. Life became the vehicle for consciousness."


  • Based on the Penrose-Hameroff quantum theory of consciousness, the theory holds that consciousness may have enabled evolution and life in the first place. Consciousness came before life.
  • Consciousness is a process of quantum self-collapses called Penrose ‘objective reductions’ (‘OR’) connected to Planck scale spacetime geometry, the fundamental level of the universe. Such events select classical states and proto-conscious qualia with ‘hedonic valence’, including pleasure.
  • Consciousness is tied to spacetime geometry, and may have existed there prior to life, and perhaps will exist there afterwards, remaining entangled and persisting in spacetime as a “quantum soul.”


If consciousness is resident in spacetime geometry rather than in neuronal computation, it implies the possibility of awareness independent of the biological body. Hameroff and Chopra hypothesize a "Quantum Soul,” suggesting that "consciousness... [is] able to exist at deeper planes and scales independent of biology." Moreover, while under normal conditions, consciousness is localized in the brain's microtubules. During end-of-life states (such as near-death experiences), the quantum information constituting consciousness may not be destroyed but rather "dissipate to the nonlocal universe at large.” Hameroff and Chopra argue that this quantum information could persist as "entangled fluctuations in multiple scales, or planes in quantum space-time geometry," offering a scientific framework, they claim, for phenomena traditionally associated with the “soul”


⭐⭐⭐ Neven's Quantum Superposition Formation


The physical substrate of consciousness is a single, precisely specified quantum-mechanical event: the formation of a superposition. The theory proposes that conscious experience is generated not by classical computation and neural information processing alone, nor by the collapse of a quantum state, but by the creation of a quantum superposition itself: “Conscious experience arises whenever a quantum mechanical superposition forms.”


  • Founded upon two convictions. First, consciousness is the one datum that cannot be eliminated. He says, “Everything starts from experience; without mind, nothing matters”. Second, classical physical descriptions seem ill-suited to explaining why experience is unified, definite, qualitative, and perhaps agentive. Quantum mechanics, by contrast, already contains superposition, entanglement, branching, basis-selection, and non-classical uncertainty—phenomena that can conceivably map onto central features of phenomenal consciousness.
  • He removes gravity. Whether gravitational influence induces collapse may be testable, but laboratory searches have so far come up empty (Donadi et al., 2021); Neven prefers classic decoherence, in which an "effective collapse" occurs when a quantum system becomes entangled with an environment of many degrees of freedom.
  • He inverts the temporal locus of experience. Where Penrose tied consciousness to collapse, Neven ties it to the birth of the superposition. The motivation, he claims, is to be consistent with governing law. If a conscious experience were shared across spatially separated entangled qubits at the moment of collapse, one could, by measuring locally, in principle, signal "faster than the speed of light".
  • 👾 When a definite state evolves unitarily into a superposition, the experiencing system follows multiple "Feynman paths" simultaneously, each yielding "a distinct sequence of conscious moments," so that a quantum system "may be composed of many experiencing minds, albeit often very simple ones".
  • The richness of an experience is bounded by the number of degrees of freedom entangled.
  • 👾 Once a quantum system reaches roughly 100 entangled qubits, an external observer cannot, even probabilistically, predict its evolution—a condition the authors liken to "Knightian uncertainty".
  • Turing machines have become intelligent but may never become conscious"; sentience, on this view, requires "a quantum Turing machine".
  • Propose an experimental framework: an “expansion protocol” that seeks to physically link a human brain to a quantum computer via quantum entanglement. This requires coupling N qubits in a subject's brain to M qubits in a processor to form an N+M superposition.
  • Neven and collaborators speculate that psychedelic, mystical, and near-death experiences may involve increased numbers of entangled qubits within the brain.


⭐⭐ Stapp’s Collapsing the Wave Function via Asking “Questions”


The quantum jump of the state of an observer's brain to the ‘Yes’ basis state (vector) then becomes the representation, in the state of that brain, of the conscious acquisition of the knowledge associated with that answer ‘Yes,’ which constitutes the neural correlate of that person's conscious experience. This fixes the essential quantum link between consciousness and neuroscience.

Mind and matter thereby become dynamically linked in a way that is causally tied to an agent's free choice of how to act. “A causal dynamical connection is established between (1) a person's conscious choices of how to act, (2) that person's consciously experienced increments in knowledge, and (3) the physical actualizations of the neural correlates of the experienced increments in knowledge”.


  • Consciousness collapses quantum wave functions by posing “questions,” making mind fundamental to physical reality.
  • Observer choices trigger quantum state collapses in the brain, linking mental events to physical outcomes.
  • Positions consciousness as an active agent, overturning classical materialism’s view of mind as inert or illusory.
  • “Mind-like” wave-function collapse that exploits quantum effects in the synapses between neurons, generating consciousness, which he believes is fundamental to the universe.


While quantum mechanics describes the evolution of potentialities for events to happen, that's all they describe, only potentialities—they do not describe what chooses the events that are going to happen, the actual events. Something must ask the questions, something outside of quantum mechanics—quantum mechanics forces that process.” The only candidate, Stapp says, must be the independent existence of consciousness.


⭐⭐ Bohm’s Implicate-Explicate Order


Everything is in a state of process or becoming (folding and unfolding)—Bohm calls it the “universal flux.” All is a dynamic, interconnected process. In the same manner, Bohm says that “knowledge, too, is a process, an abstraction from the one total flux, which latter is therefore the ground both of reality and of knowledge of this reality”.


  • Reality has two intertwined orders: the visible explicate order and the hidden implicate order.
  • Mind and matter share a common higher-dimensional ground beyond either’s independent existence.
  • Suggests a unified, dynamic wholeness linking consciousness and matter in continuous transformation.
  • In mechanistic order, which is inherent to classical physics, “the principal feature of this order is that the world is regarded as constituted of entities which are outside of each other, in the sense that they exist independently in different regions of space (and time) and interact through forces that do not bring about any changes in their essential natures. The machine gives a typical illustration of such a system of order… By contrast, in a living organism, for example, each part grows in the context of the whole, so that it does not exist independently, nor can it be said that it merely ‘interacts’ with the others, without itself being essentially affected in this relationship”.


⭐⭐ Chalmers and McQueen’s Quantum Collapse


“The most common reason among philosophers for rejecting property dualist theories of consciousness is an argument from physics. This argument runs roughly as follows: (1) every physical effect has only physical causes, (2) consciousness causes physical effects, so (3) consciousness is physical. The key first premise is a causal closure thesis, supported by the observation that there are no causal gaps in standard physics that a nonphysical consciousness might fill.”

“Wave function collapse in quantum mechanics appears to be precisely such a gap, and consciousness-collapse models are at least not obviously ruled out by known physics.”

  • Consciousness may trigger quantum wave function collapse, bridging gaps in physics and mind-body theory.
  • Combines Integrated Information Theory with quantum collapse dynamics to link conscious states to collapse events.


⭐⭐⭐ Wolfram’s Consciousness in the Ruliad


Physicist and computer scientist Stephen Wolfram seeks “to formalize issues about consciousness, and to turn questions about consciousness into what amounts to concrete questions about mathematics, computation, logic or whatever that can be formally and rigorously explored”. He begins by embedding consciousness in what he calls the “ruliad” (neologism from “rules”), which he defines as “the entangled limit of everything that is computationally possible: the result of following all possible computational rules in all possible ways.” The ruliad, he says, is “a kind of ultimate limit of all abstraction and generalization,” encapsulating “not only all formal possibilities but also everything about our physical universe”. The ruliad is crucial for formalizing the “rules” of consciousness, he argues, because “everything we experience can be thought of as sampling that part of the ruliad that corresponds to our particular way of perceiving and interpreting the universe”.


  • Consciousness is our coherent, sequential experience formed by sampling the “ruliad,” the space of all possible computations.
  • Consciousness is not maximal computation but a “step down” enabling coherent perception of a branching universe.
  • Could different consciousnesses meet by moving through both physical and rulial space?
  • Quantum mechanics emerges “as a result of trying to form a coherent perception of the universe”—Wolfram offers a sharp epigram to describe consciousness: “how branching brains perceive a branching universe”.


⭐⭐ Faggin’s Quantum Information-Based Panpsychism


Faggin’s core concept is “seities”—fundamental quantum-conscious agents that existed prior to the existence of spacetime and which form reality via interaction between two spaces: C-space, the “private conscious experiences of the seities that collectively make up the subjective inner world of experience,” and I-space, “the symbolic space of live information constituting the objective vibrational space shared by all seities.” C-space and I-space, Faggin says, “are indivisible and represent the two subjective-objective, semantic-symbolic, and inner-outer aspects of the basic reality of the communicating seities. These spaces are extra-physical and do not need to be tridimensional.”


  • Consciousness is an irreducible property of nature, intrinsic to quantum systems in pure states.
  • Pure quantum states mirror conscious experience through their definiteness, privacy, and integration of qualia.
  • A quantum state is an effective mathematical representation of a conscious experience because it possesses the same crucial characteristics of what it represents: the definiteness and privacy of the experience.
  • Like free will and consciousness, “are non-algorithmic properties that can only exist in a fundamental layer of the universe ruled by quantum physics.


⭐⭐ Kauffman’s Mind Mediating Possibles to Actuals


(i) Quantum measurement converts Res potentia—ontologically real Possibles—into Res extensa - ontologically real Actuals. (ii) Brain/mind/consciousness cannot be purely classical physics because no classical system can be an analog computer whose dynamic behavior can be isomorphic to “possible uses”, and therefore, brain/mind/consciousness must be partly quantum.


  • Mind, partly quantum, collapses the wave function, turning potential states into experienced realities.
  • Mind is nonlocal and plays an active causal role in the physical world.


⭐⭐⭐ Carr’s Higher Dimensions and Mental Space


  • Consciousness is a fundamental aspect of reality requiring a higher-dimensional “mental space” beyond spacetime.
  • Marries matter and mind from the universe’s beginning, not as one emerging from the other.
  • The brain limits experience, while mind operates within an expanded space linking minds and matter.
  • Could explain paranormal and transpersonal experiences, demanding physics expand to include consciousness.
  • Physical reality consists of more than just the four-dimensional spacetime of Einstein's relativity. To fully account for consciousness—including paranormal or transpersonal experiences—he argues that a higher-dimensional space is necessary. Carr draws on hyperspace theories (from string theory and Kaluza-Klein theory), evidence from psi/parapsychology (e.g., telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition), and the phenomenology of altered state.
  • Carr sees support from psi, or the paranormal. While he recognizes that psi “encompasses a multitude of sins,” there are some aspects, such as telepathy and clairvoyance, which he takes seriously, whereas other aspects, such as precognition and psychokinesis, less so. Still, he regards even these psi phenomena as possible because of potential deep interactions between consciousness and physics.


Consciousness is just an epiphenomenon produced by the brain, independent of physics, and that as physicists they don't have to confront the problem of consciousness because, after all, physics has a third-person perspective, objects in the outside world, whereas consciousness has a first-person perspective. In other words, clearly brains exist and brains are physical systems, but consciousness is simply beyond the domain of physics. The real issue is how can physics ever accommodate that first-person perspective?


⭐⭐ Smolin’s Causal Theory of Views


Smolin bases his theory on two concepts: First, the beables of a relational theory to be the views of events. Second, the possibility of making a physical distinction between common and routine states, on the one hand, and novel and unique states, on the other. “A relational theory that incorporates both ideas offers a possible setting for bringing qualia and consciousness into physics. The physical correlates of consciousness would be the novel or unique views of events”.

“Natural to propose that instances or moments of conscious experience are aspects of some views. That is, an elementary unit of consciousness is not a single qualia, but the entire of a partial view of the universe, as seen from one event”.


  • Consciousness arises from rare, novel “views” of events within a relational universe of causal perspectives.
  • Restricts panpsychism to a small set of rare, non-duplicated event views, tying qualia to physical novelty.
  • Each event has a “view” from its causal past; unprecedented, unique views correlate with conscious moments.
  • Offers a realist, physics-based selection principle linking qualia to the structure and history of the universe.
  • Relational “hidden variables” theory: a realist description of precisely what goes on in each individual event or process, which reduces to quantum mechanics in a certain limit and averaging procedure.


⭐⭐ Pylkkänen’s Quantum Potential Energy and Active Information


  • Consciousness fits more naturally in a Bohm-inspired quantum framework where matter contains “active information.”
  • Rejects purely mechanical physics, proposing non-mechanical, organic concepts to bridge neurophysiology and consciousness.
  • Quantum reality includes a new kind of potential energy that guides systems holistically, influencing mind–matter links.
  • Opens the door for genuine mental causation without violating physics, challenging epiphenomenalism.


⭐⭐⭐ Keppler’s Zero-Point Field


  • Consciousness is a fundamental feature of the universe, rooted in the quantum vacuum’s zero-point field.
  • Treats the ZPF as a universal “palette” of qualia, with brain activity acting like a resonant filter.
  • The brain resonates with specific ZPF modes, selectively amplifying them to produce conscious states.
  • Suggests neurotransmitter control of ZPF coupling could explain shifts in awareness and guide new research.


Keppler brings together his views of the neurophysiology of consciousness and of quantum field theory. From the perspective of the neurophysiology, he relies on an electromagnetic field theory of consciousness. “Empirical findings indicate that conscious states are linked to long-range synchronized activity patterns that result from phase transitions and exhibit the key features of self-organized criticality”. From the perspective of quantum field theory, he focuses on the vacuum state of quantum electrodynamics (QED), an essential ingredient of which is “a fluctuating ocean of energy, the ubiquitous electromagnetic zero-point field (ZPF), consisting of a spectrum of normal modes”.


⭐⭐⭐ Strømme’s Universal Consciousness as Foundational Field


Consciousness is not an emergent property of neural processes, but is a fundamental field of reality from which space–time, matter, and individual minds emerge. It is a non-reductive, non-dual, cosmological theory, where solving the mind–brain problem requires reframing the ultimate, deep structural relation among consciousness, physics, individuality, and reality as a whole.


  • Mind as “the universal creative intelligence,” the source and driver of differentiation; Consciousness as “the universal capacity for awareness,” the field in which space, time, and matter are realized; and Thought as “the creative mechanism” by which formless potential becomes structured, subjective reality. These three principles are not ordinary psychological descriptions. “Universal thought,” especially, is “not personal intellectual thought” but a formless, creative principle by which the undifferentiated attains form.
  • The theory leverages conceptual and mathematical ideas from quantum field theory, quantum measurement, and cosmology to explain, or at least suggest, how unity becomes plurality. Three mechanisms are notable. First, symmetry breaking: the consciousness field begins in an undifferentiated, symmetric state, then shifts into structured states. In physics, symmetry breaking explains how a uniform field can generate distinct particles, forces, or phases. In Strømme’s model, it functions as an analogy for how universal consciousness differentiates into worlds and minds. Second, quantum fluctuations: small perturbations in the primordial field seed structure. Just as quantum fluctuations in early-universe cosmology (e.g., inflation theory) are invoked to explain the emergence of large-scale cosmic structure, fluctuations in the consciousness field may initiate differentiation into physical and experiential forms. Third, projection or self-reflection: universal consciousness may, through universal thought, actualize certain possibilities rather than others. Universal thought is explicitly “not personal intellectual thought,” but a creative principle by which potential becomes actuality. She invokes these novel ways of thinking as conceptual bridges between physics and non-dual metaphysics.
  • Personal thought becomes the mechanism by which individualized consciousness constructs lived reality. It selects, organizes, and interprets experience from moment to moment. This creates personal identity, meaning, attention, emotion, and world-representation. It also reinforces the “illusion of separateness”: the felt conviction that each subject is an isolated consciousness enclosed within a body, rather than a localized expression of a universal field.
  • If consciousness is field-like, it may have detectable effects in diverse expressions: quantum-sensitive systems, biological coherence, brain synchronization, random-number generator anomalies, collective emotional events, or even cosmological traces.


⭐⭐⭐ Beck-Eccles’s Quantum Processes in the Synapse


  • Conscious intentions can influence brain activity by biasing quantum events at neuronal synapses.
  • Links free will to quantum uncertainty in calcium ion dynamics at synaptic trigger sites.
  • Mental volition alters the probability of neurotransmitter release via quantum state reduction during exocytosis.
  • Suggests consciousness can directly shape neural activity, challenging strictly mechanistic brain models.


Beck and Eccles propose that “the quantum state reduction, or selection of amplitudes, offers a doorway for a new logic, the quantum logic, with its unpredictability for a single event.” Because conscious action (e.g., intention) is a dynamical process which forms temporal patterns in relevant areas of the brain (cerebral cortex), they propose how regulating the myriad synaptic switches between innumerable neurons in those relevant areas can be regulated effectively by a quantum trigger (based on an electron transfer process in the synaptic membrane). Thus, they conclude, “conscious action is essentially related to quantum state reduction”.


  • 👾 Stapp supports the hypothesis that quantum effects are important in brain dynamics in connection with cerebral exocytosis. Exocytosis is instigated by a neuronal action potential pulse that triggers an influx of calcium ions through ion channels into a nerve terminal, such that, due to the very small diameter of the ion channel, the quantum wave packet that describes the location of the ion spreads out to a size much larger than the trigger site. This means that “one must retain both the possibility that the ion activates the trigger, and exocytosis occurs, and also the possibility that the ion misses the trigger site, and exocytosis does not occur”. As Beck and Eccles hypothesize, “the mental intention (the volition) becomes neurally effective by momentarily increasing the probability of exocytosis in selected cortical areas”.


⭐⭐⭐ Fisher’s Quantum Cognition


  • Quantum processing with nuclear spins might be operative in the brain and key to its functioning. He identifies “phosphorus as the unique biological element with a nuclear spin that can serve as a qubit for such putative quantum processing—a neural qubit—while the phosphate ion is the only possible qubit-transporter.”
  • Consciousness may involve quantum computation in the brain using phosphorus nuclear spins as neural qubits.
  • Experimental proof of Posner molecule quantum effects in living brains is still lacking.
  • Phosphorus atoms in “Posner molecules” preserve entanglement, influencing neuron firing via calcium release.
  • Points to a possible bridge between quantum biology and neuroscience, sparking a new “quantum neuroscience” field.
  • 👾 “Posner molecule” (calcium phosphate clusters, Ca9(PO4)6) as “the unique molecule that can protect the neural qubits on very long times and thereby serve as a (working) quantum-memory”. “The dynamics and quantum entanglement of the phosphorus nuclear spins must be capable of modulating the excitability and signaling of neurons”—which he takes as a working definition of “quantum cognition”.


⭐⭐ Rourk’s Catecholaminergic Neuron Electron Transport Theory


  • The Catecholaminergic Neuron Electron Transport (CNET) theory proposes that phenomenal consciousness is associated with a quantum-biological neural signaling mechanism—in catecholaminergic systems of the midbrain’s substantia nigra pars compacta (SNc) and locus coeruleus (LC), where ferritin- and neuromelanin-mediated electron transport helps bind, select, and amplify cortical and sensory signals organized through basal-ganglia action-selection channels.
  • The theory’s distinctive claim is not that consciousness is a free-floating quantum process, nor that quantum mechanics directly explains subjectivity as such, but that a specific quantum-biological signaling substrate in identified neural tissue may help solve the neurobiological problem of how globally available information becomes unified, salient, and action-relevant.
  • The prefrontal cortex is especially important because it helps organize signals to basal ganglia loops, helping to form the structured channels through which conscious, action-guiding content may emerge.
  • Conductive atomic-force microscopy has reportedly indicated quantum-mechanical electron transport in human substantia nigra tissue in regions enriched in ferritin and neuromelanin, but not in nearby control tissue.
  • The theory could have implications for free will and agency, because conscious volition would depend partly on how candidate action channels are formed, amplified, and selected.


⭐⭐ Cavelier's Entangled Spins at the NMDA Receptor


  • Conscious experience could be registered in the brain by being embedded in entangled radical-pair spin states associated with N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptors, and that the information so embedded stands in a biconditional relation (each obtaining if and only if the other does) with the spike-frequency and timing codes of the associated neural circuits.
  • Electromagnetic fields within the brain arise from neurons, their components, and the surrounding environment; scalp electroencephalography (EEG) captures the external aggregate, while microelectrode measurements are confined to the immediate vicinity of membranes, dendrites, synapses, and axons. Intracranial electroencephalography, developed for epilepsy monitoring, now permits measurement of internal fields at near-cellular resolution with high temporal precision, including during episodes of unconsciousness. Cavelier reads the emerging electromagnetic-field literature as converging on an account of qualia in which synchronized fields across the whole brain, rather than spiking within isolated circuits, underwrite functional unity—and argues that such an account must be extended downward to nanoscale processes grounded in fundamental physics.
  • Entanglement as a candidate mechanism for consciousness is drawn from recent work in that area, and the open question of at which stage biological relevance would arise—entanglement onset, coherent evolution, or decoherence and collapse—connects to concurrent efforts to test whether quantum processes create conscious experience.


⭐⭐⭐ Shiah’s Cryptochrome Theory


  • Consciousness can directly influence the physical world through quantum processes mediated by the protein cryptochrome.
  • Identifies a specific molecule and quantum mechanism linking mind, biology, and reported psi phenomena.
  • Cryptochromes act as biological transducers, converting consciousness-driven information into physical effects via radical-pair quantum reactions.
  • Suggests consciousness is a non-local force, with potential applications in biology, medicine, and future communication technologies.
  • The theory argues that a quiet state of consciousness (such as meditation) facilitates psi because it reduces "thermal noise" in the body. By lowering core body temperature and somatic noise, the effectiveness of the radical-pair mechanism is enhanced, allowing the subtle "signal" of psi to be more easily detecte.
  • The Cryptochrome Theory aligns scientific inquiry with Buddhist wisdom, particularly the idea that achieving a "nonself" or "contentless" state of consciousness naturally awakens psi abilities. Shiah suggests that by understanding the energetic basis of these phenomena, we can develop new technologies for medicine, agriculture, and even long-distance communication in space travel.


⭐⭐ Torday’s Cellular and Cosmic Consciousness


  • Consciousness emerges from cellular physiology, linking quantum processes in cells to cosmic-scale awareness.
  • The cell acts like a Möbius strip, seamlessly uniting “inside” biology with the wider cosmos.
  • How physiological quantum entanglement translates into subjective experience remains speculative.
  • Symbiogenesis and quantum entanglement connect local cellular memories with non-local cosmic consciousness.
  • Suggests consciousness is continuous from physiology to universal mind, challenging strict materialism.
  • Quantum entanglement is integral to our physiology, and that it links our local consciousness with the non-local consciousness of the cosmos, distinguishing causation from coincidence based on science.


👾 The symbiogenic mechanism for assimilation of factors in the environment formulates the continuum from inside the cell to the cosmos, both consciousness and cosmology complying with the laws of nature. Since symbiogenesis is ‘constructive’, whereas eliminating what threatens us is ‘destructive’, why do we largely practice symbiogenesis? Hypothetically, symbiogenesis recursively simulates the monism of our origin, recognizing ‘something bigger than ourselves’." (...) “Symbiogenesis is recognized as the means by which both local and non-local consciousness have evolved, the former based on the homeostatic control of energy in sync with the environment, the latter as the higher-order mathematical scaffolding of our physiology connecting homeostasis with the mathematics of the cosmos”.


⭐⭐⭐ Globus’s Quantum Thermofield Brain Dynamic


An application of quantum field theory to brain functioning, which he calls “quantum brain dynamics,” and the continental postphenomenological tradition, especially the work of Martin Heidegger and Jacques Derrida. Underlying both, he says, “is a new ontology of non-Cartesian dual modes whose rich provenance is their between".

“The problematics of consciousness/brain, qualia, and measurement in quantum physics are resolved by substituting disclosure for perceptual consciousness and distinguishing the phenomenal brain-p from the macroscopic quantum object brain-q”.


  • The brain discloses the phenomenal world through dissipative quantum thermofield dynamics, not via perceptual consciousness.
  • Merges quantum brain theory with Heideggerian and Derridean philosophy into a non-Cartesian, disclosure-based ontology.
  • Highly abstract; lacks empirical pathways to test the philosophical-quantum synthesis it proposes.
  • Brain states (“brain-p” phenomenal, “brain-q” quantum) are triply tuned by senses, memory, and self-adjustment to create reality.
  • Reframes the hard problem and quantum measurement problem as issues of disclosure, bypassing standard consciousness models.


⭐⭐ Poznanski’s Dynamic Organicity Theory


Dynamic Organicity Theory (DOT) of consciousness, a quantum biological theory based on a multiscale interpretation of type-B materialism. DOT utilizes a multiscalar temporal-topological framework to include quantum biological effects in the sense of what happens to macroscopic systems upon interaction with quantum potential energy that exists when a living negentropic state of the brain imposes thermodynamic constraints.

DOT does not deal with quantum consciousness or assume quantum brain dynamics. However, a Schrödinger-like equation describes the quantum effects within the multiscale complexity, where multiscale complexity is both functional and structural through changeable boundary conditions (resulting in the topology being a holarchical modularity). This is made possible by treating time consciousness, i.e., “consciousness-in-the-moment,” on a nonlinear temporal scale and implicitly grounding space to the contingency of changing boundary conditions. The approach is based on the dynamics of functional relations (not to be confused with functionalist or relational theories of consciousness). It is a nonspatial topological framework (not the mathematical study of “space” in a general sense of topological spaces) associated with the temporal aspect of the functionality.


  • Consciousness emerges from quantum-delocalized, self-referential information systems in living brains, grounded in quasi-strong emergence.
  • Replaces “emergence” with a self-reference principle, focusing on functional pathways over structural descriptions.
  • The FMultiComplexity > FMaxComplexity threshold is conceptually bold but awaits empirical verification in biological systems.
  • Multiscale, holarchical brain dynamics integrate quantum-level effects with changing boundary conditions to create intentional agency.
  • Challenges panpsychism and pure functionalism, framing experience as embodied, enacted, and tied to negentropic brain states.


⭐⭐ Gambini and Pullin’s Quantum Panprotopsychism


  • Consciousness arises from quantum events with intrinsic phenomenal aspects, a form of panprotopsychism.
  • Solves the subject-summing “combination problem” by appealing to quantum indeterminacy and causal efficacy of consciousness.
  • Quantum states and events carry internal phenomenal qualities; entanglement generates emergent properties beyond classical physics.
  • Offers a physics-grounded ontology where consciousness is neither epiphenomenal nor reducible, linking quantum processes to selfhood.


⭐⭐⭐ King’s Symbiotic Existential Cosmology


Subjective consciousness and the physical universe have an irreducible, asymmetric, complementary interactive relationship in which life is a biospherically symbiotic climax phenomenon in the mature universe. Interactivity is key to cosmology, affirming the ability of subjective consciousness to participate in causal intent and acausal anticipation through coupled, physically indeterminate phase transition states, which are seen as key to why subjective consciousness has been preserved by biological evolution over computational biology.


  • Subjective consciousness forms an irreducible, interactive complement to the physical universe, enabled by critical phase transition states in both brain dynamics and cosmology.
  • This paradigm-shifting theory challenges scientific zeitgeist, asserting that subjective conscious agency provides a uniquely privileged window into the universe.
  • The climactic unfolding of Earth's biosphere is cosmologically sacrosanct—a privileged, protected outcome of universal evolution.


Quantum wave-particle complementarity is an irreducible interactive topological relationship between continuous and discrete processes. Consciousness-universe complementarity—between the subdivisible quantum universe and the holistic stream of subjective consciousness—also has topological irreducibility. Consciousness and mental causation as a double topological world knot, after Schopenhauer’s ‘inexplicable’ Weltknoten—how the willing ‘I’ can be both the generator of the world of appearances it experiences and a particular being within that world, implying that primal subjectivity is foundational to cosmology.


⭐⭐⭐ Pacheco’s Science of Unity


👾 The “Science of Unity” model posits matter and consciousness as deeply intertwined, following a fractal-like hierarchical arrangement from fundamental forms to macro-structures. Reality unfolds across higher frequential dimensions, where different Standard Models coexist within the same space but experience distinct physical properties.


  • Matter and consciousness form a single fractal continuum, differing only in density and vibrational state.
  • Introduces a “non-causal dimension” of mathematical indeterminacy, enabling genuine free will beyond determinism.
  • The coexistence of multiple Standard Models in parallel frequency realities remains speculative and untested.
  • Consciousness filters through layered densities in an inverted fractal pattern, shaped by structural and perceptual shifts.
  • As matter organizes into progressively refined states, consciousness interacts dynamically with this evolving structure. It is not an emergent phenomenon but a fundamental aspect of reality, following an inverted fractal pattern that progressively filters awareness as it moves through different densities. This process defines levels of consciousness, transitioning from omniscience to intuitive, rational, instinctive, automated, and ultimately total unconsciousness.


⭐⭐⭐ Tozzi’s Multidimensional Brain


  • Brain activity unfolds in high-dimensional spaces beyond the familiar three spatial dimensions plus time.
  • Higher dimensions may be real anatomical/functional properties, not just abstract mathematical tools.
  • Uncertainty remains over whether extra dimensions are ontological realities or modeling conveniences.
  • Two core arguments. First, additional dimensions serve as powerful methodological constructs that enhance the mathematical modeling of complex neurodynamics. Second, and more provocatively, he says, these dimensions may correspond to real, albeit hidden, anatomical and functional properties of the brain.
  • Disorders such as Parkinson’s, Huntington’s, and schizophrenia show disruptions in connectome topologies that, when examined in higher-dimensional spaces, may yield diagnostic and therapeutic insights. Further, psychedelic-induced states such as those caused by LSD are explored as empirical windows into high-dimensional cognition, potentially driven by increased global neural integration.


⭐⭐ Morrison’s Position Selecting Interactionism


  • Consciousness correlates with the wavefunction of a single rare particle confined within a neuron’s “cavity.”
  • Subjective images form when sensory systems modulate the particle’s wavefunction amplitude in distinct ways for different qualia.
  • Requires a yet-unidentified rare particle and mechanisms that ensure that only one particle of this type is present in a neuron-sized cavity.
  • Particle position-measurements, influenced by sensory inputs, memories, and emotion, direct shifts in attention.
  • Explains attention’s randomness, the “spotlight” effect, and how qualia bind into unified perceptual scenes.
  • The qualia we happen to experience must be those associated with certain ‘particles’ in brain environments that have evolved through natural selection to be well-adapted to providing representations of the outside world. Consciousness does not emerge out of nowhere. The potential for our qualia lies in the basic nature of matter, but it has taken millions of years for arrangements of matter so well suited to representing an outside world to arise through natural selection.


⭐⭐⭐ Van Dusen’s Hyperspatial Dimension Theory


Dimensional theory of consciousness proposes that mind is not contained within ordinary four-dimensional physical space-time, but is identical with operations in real higher-dimensional spaces. Phenomenal consciousness, on this view, is not produced by the brain; it is the differentiated “line of awareness” running through these levels, with the brain serving as its lower physical anchor.

✨👾 Four dimensions are inadequate to explain mind, and constructs an alternative with topology, mathematical logic, and group theory: for any n-space, the boundary of an arbitrary point must be (n−1)-dimensional, and n must be the least integer sufficient. Building upward, a four-space entity is a moving solid—an event. Crucially, a series of events cannot be found in the physical world, since any cut in physical happening yields a solid, not an event; physical events are always four-dimensional. The only place such a lumping together of differently dated events occurs is mind. 👾✨


⭐⭐ QBism’s Primitive, Participatory Experience: Quantum Agency and the First-Person World


Qbism is an interpretation of quantum mechanics with implications for consciousness, rather than a quantum-neural mechanism that generates consciousness.


  • QBism begins with personalist Bayesian probability. Probabilities—including quantum probabilities—are an individual agent’s degrees of belief concerning possible future experiences, not objective propensities residing in physical systems. Consequently, the wavefunction or density operator does not describe an objectively existing physical condition. Even a probability-one assignment expresses maximal personal certainty rather than an element of reality guaranteed to exist before measurement.
  • The conceptual sequence is: agent → action upon world → unpredictable consequence → personal experience → revised expectations.


⭐⭐ Rovelli’s Relational Physics


Although there is nothing specifically psychic or mental in the relational properties of a system with respect to another system. There is definitely something in common with panpsychism, because the world is not described from the outside: it is always described relative to a physical system. So, physical reality is, in our current physics, perspectival reality.


  • Physics describes relations between systems, not intrinsic properties, making reality inherently perspectival.
  • Relationalism reduces the gap between mental and physical by reframing both as perspective-dependent.
  • Subjective experience still seems more private than physics’ relational descriptions allow.
  • Entities manifest only through interactions, with all descriptions relative to specific physical systems.
  • May offer a mild form of panpsychism while deflating the “hard problem” of consciousness.


⚛️ Information


Theories that posit information as ontological (not just descriptive) in generating phenomenal consciousness. Many of these theories have information as the fundamental substance of reality, more fundamental than matter-energy and spacetime, and consciousness arises from, or is identical with, informational structures or processes.


⭐⭐ Tononi’s Integrated Information Theory


  • Consciousness is identical to a maximally integrated cause–effect structure (“Φ-structure”) in physical systems.
  • Treats consciousness as an intrinsic structural property of reality, not as a function or computation.
  • Sufficiency of Φ for consciousness is disputed; critics question empirical support and ontological claims.
  • Starts from universal properties of experience, formalizes them into physical postulates, and measures integration as Φ.
  • Maximally irreducible intrinsically, it is also known as a “quale” (plural: qualia). Its arguably infinite varieties are formed when higher-order mechanisms specify concepts, with the constellation of all concepts specifying the overall form or shape of the quale.
  • True free will exists, based on “the proper understanding of experience as true existence and on the intrinsic powers view: what truly exists, in physical terms, are intrinsic entities, and only what truly exists can cause.” In contrast, in materialistic theories, with ontological and causal micro-determination, much of the debate about free will has revolved not around existence but around determinism/indeterminism, so that true free will is incompatible.
  • Offers a measurable test for consciousness, predicts brain regions involved, and claims true free will is possible.


  • Each of IIT's five postulates defines and constrains the properties required of physical mechanisms to support consciousness:

1️⃣ Intrinsic Existence. Consciousness exists of its own inherent nature: each experience is real, and it exists from its own inherent perspective; to account for experience, a system of mechanisms in a state must exist intrinsically, and it must have cause–effect power.
2️⃣ Composition. Consciousness is structured: each experience is composed of phenomenological distinctions; the system must be structured: subsets of system elements (composed in various combinations) must have cause–effect power upon the system.
3️⃣ Information. Consciousness is specific: each experience is the particular way it is; the system must specify a cause–effect-enabling structure that is the particular way it is; the system has a set of specific cause–effect repertoires that distinguishes it from all other possible structures (differentiation).
4️⃣ Integration. Consciousness is unified: each experience is irreducible to noninterdependent subsets of phenomenal distinctions; the cause–effect structure specified by the system must be unified: it must be intrinsically irreducible.
5️⃣ Exclusion. Consciousness is definite in content and spatio-temporal grain: each experience has the set of phenomenal distinctions it has, not less or more, and flows at the speed it does, not faster or slower; the cause–effect structure specified by the system must be definite and is maximally irreducible intrinsically (“conceptual structure”).


⭐⭐ Chalmers’s Double-Aspect Theory of Information


  • Information is fundamental and has both a physical and an experiential aspect.
  • Extends neutral monism, linking Spinoza’s and Russell’s ideas to a naturalistic dualism.
  • Offers information as the “common currency” bridging physical processes and consciousness.
  • “This treatment of information brings out a crucial link between the physical and the phenomenal: whenever we find an information space realized phenomenally, we find the same information space realized physically. And when an experience realizes an information state, the same information state is realized in the experience’s physical substrate.” In other words, “Wherever there is conscious experience, there is information; where there is information, there is a potential for conscious experience… We can think of information as the common currency between the physical and the phenomenal”.


⭐⭐⭐ Davies’s Hidden Web of Information


  • Information may be more fundamental than matter or energy — a view shared by others like John Archibald Wheeler (“It from bit”) and more recently in some quantum information theories. This view holds that the universe is best understood not as made of particles or forces, but as made of information. This sets the stage for applying informational principles to explain life and mind.
  • The laws of physics might somehow “know” to bring about life and consciousness, a notion related to his ideas on quant histories and teleological principles or law-like behavior shaped by informational constraints. “We may be missing a crucial layer of reality, one that lies between the level of physical law and the observed complexity of life. This layer may involve information as a new sort of causal agent.” Davies speculates that this “middle layer” might be where consciousness lies.
  • Proposes a “middle layer” of informational causation between physics and biological complexity.
  • Suggests science itself begins in consciousness, making mind central to understanding the cosmos.
  • Consciousness could emerge from or interact with deep informational structures underlying physical law.
  • Conscious observers may influence the universe’s history, echoing Wheeler’s participatory anthropic principle.


⭐⭐⭐ Levin’s Ingressing Minds from Platonic Space


✨👾 The “Ingressing Minds from Platonic Space” framework proposes that phenomenal consciousness is not generated by brains as self-sufficient, wholly physical devices, but rather, like mathematical truth and biological form, "ingresses" into the physical world from a structured, non-physical "latent [Platonic] space" of patterns—a space that contains not only “low-agency patterns” such as facts about triangles and prime numbers but also high-agency cognitive patterns such as "kinds of minds." 👾✨


  • The relationship between mind and brain "is the same as the relationship between mathematical patterns and the morphogenetic outcomes they guide".
  • "Minds are the patterns.” Bodies—brains, embryos, biobots, AI systems, hybrid organisms—are the material conditions that permit such patterns to enter physical history. This is why the theory is distinctive among consciousness theories: it treats phenomenal consciousness neither as neural computation, nor as biological emergence, nor as intrinsic matter-wide mentality alone, but as the experiential projection of nonphysical, structured, agential patterns into physical embodiments.
  • If low-agency mathematical forms can shape physical outcomes, perhaps high-agency forms—“kinds of minds”—can also ingress into physical systems.


👾 “Platonic space” is not a catalog of static abstractions. Levin’s more radical proposal is that it contains dynamic and perhaps agential structures. Logical systems can oscillate, networks can learn, gene-regulatory models can show memory-like capacities, and minimal formal systems can exhibit surprisingly rich behavior. These are not yet conscious minds; they are conceptual footholds for imagining a graded ontology of patterns—from inert mathematical regularities to active cognitive agents.

👾 If novel constructs reveal novel goal-directed patterns, then future experiments might ask whether some structures enable not only behavior but rudimentary valence, preference, memory, or perspective. The same logic extends—cautiously—to AI and robotics. Levin does not equate language models with human minds; he argues that whether engineered systems instantiate genuine mindedness should be an empirical question, not a philosophical determination.


⭐⭐ Hoel’s Causal Emergence


  • ‘Causal emergence,’ the idea that macroscopic structures in the brain can have stronger or clearer causal power than their microscopic components and that consciousness arises where macro-level causal structure is maximized. “Consciousness is what it’s like for information to be causally emergent.”
  • Consciousness arises where macro-level causal structures have greater causal power than their micro-level parts.
  • Experience occurs at the level where causal emergence peaks, often structured as a narrative mind.
  • Links consciousness, free will, and meaning to the “world behind the world” of emergent structures.
  • Higher-scale brain patterns maximize “effective information,” giving them unique causal efficacy.
  • Rejects the idea that neurons alone explain mind; emergent stories and meanings are causally real.


Causal emergence theory as the philosophical foundation for not only consciousness, but also for free will and meaning. He claims to solve the hard problem of consciousness, which is not, he says, a byproduct of physicalism, but the peak of a causal pyramid, where emergent structures like narratives, identities, and cultures play foundational roles. The “world behind the world”—the title of his book—is the place where these higher-order patterns reside, and recognizing their causal efficacy is key to understanding both consciousness and reality itself.


⭐⭐ Doyle’s Experience Recorder and Reproducer


  • Consciousness arises from replaying past experiences—sensory and emotional—when current events resemble them.
  • ERR solves the “binding problem” by storing sensory and emotional data already integrated.
  • Neural assemblies store experiences with linked emotions, which are re-presented as unified wholes.
  • Treats information as having both physical and phenomenal aspects, potentially linking brain and mind.


⭐⭐ Langan’s Cognitive-Theoretic Model of the Universe


  • Reality is a self-contained, self-processing system uniting mind, matter, and meaning into one reflexive framework.
  • Consciousness is a generic property of reality, with all minds nested within the eternal “mind of God.”
  • Reality necessarily generates itself from possibility alone, requiring nothing outside itself to exist.
  • A “self-simulation” language (telesis) generates and models reality from within, coupling the physical and mental.
  • Claims to unify physics, logic, and spirituality, proving God’s existence and outlining moral consequences.


⭐⭐ Boyd’s Emergent Information Theory


Emergent Information Theory (EIT) bridges the mind-body gap by proposing that the brain creates a “non-material mind” (as defined in an informational context) that is capable of influencing events in the physical body. As such, EIT can be considered as an alternative form of emergent dualism that unifies all biological and technological information systems.


  • Consciousness arises from non-physical informational structures generated by the brain, not from physical matter alone.
  • Introduces a non-material “information dimension” where consciousness and other emergent phenomena reside.
  • Hierarchical information patterns built from neuronal states exert top-down causal control over physical brain activity.
  • While both brains and computers process information, only biological systems self-organize to produce consciousness.


⭐⭐⭐ Resch’s Platonic Functionalism


Consciousness, reality, and computation are deeply integrated at a fundamental level. In philosophy of mind, functionalism says it's how a mind works that matters, not what stuff it's made of. In philosophy of mathematics, Platonism says that mathematical objects have an independent existence. Functionalism and Platonism are both leading theories in their respective fields, but when they are taken together, something extraordinary happens.


  • Consciousness depends only on functional organization, and all possible computations exist as real entities.
  • Physical reality emerges from computation, making minds and worlds Platonic structures with lawful order.
  • Suggests we may be computational “doppelgängers” in a mathematical universe, erasing the need for separate matter.
  • Functionalism’s substrate-independence plus Platonism’s real mathematics imply every computation—and mind—exists.
  • Computational models from multiple researchers predict quantum, relativistic, and thermodynamic laws.


  • When it comes to deciding whether functionalism is true, he says, we're faced with the following four propositions:

1️⃣ No computer program can emulate the human brain.
2️⃣ An emulation is possible, but it wouldn't be conscious.
3️⃣ The emulation would be conscious, but not in the same way.
4️⃣ The emulation would be conscious in exactly the same way.
5️⃣ We have cause to doubt (1) because all known physical laws are computable, and also because we have biologically accurate models of neurons that don't assume any exotic non-computable physics.

➡️ We have cause to doubt (2) because it makes consciousness into a pointless epiphenomenon, with no reason to evolve; furthermore, it makes scenarios like the zombie universe possible, wherein philosophers write books about consciousness without being conscious.
➡️ We have cause to doubt (3) because, as Chalmers shows, it would mean qualia could dance and fade without one noticing (Chalmers, 1995a), or, as Zuboff's partial visual cortex replacement argument shows, one could have inverted colors in half of their visual field without any impairment in function (Zuboff, 1994).
➡️ If we reject the first three possibilities, then only one option remains. We thereby conclude that an emulation of a human brain would not only be conscious but must be conscious in the exact same way as a functionally equivalent biological brain. Thus, Resch concludes, functional organization fully defines the character of both consciousness and qualia.


👾 Since the set of all mathematical objects includes every computation (Jones, 1978), it follows that among this set of all computations is a computation equivalent to a full simulation of the history of our universe, accurate to the detail of every particle interaction. Such a computation contains the full history of our universe, the formation of galaxies, the evolution of life, and the rise of civilization. It will also contain observers who, Resch argues, will be just as conscious as you and I: “They will talk about consciousness, write books about qualia and the hard problem, and even spend time reading articles about the mysteries of consciousness”.

👾 If functionalism is true, Resch says, these functional duplicates must be as conscious and self-aware as we are. But if there is no difference in consciousness between us, who exist physically, and our “computational doppelgängers,” who exist Platonically, who is to say we're not, ourselves, these computational doppelgängers? If we could be them, then any need to assume a separately existing physical world vanishes. Applying Occam's razor, we can drop the assumption of a separate physical world, and we're left with a monism based on Platonically existing computation.

👾 “The basic idea has been around since the time of Pythagoras, who said 'everything is number'. Now, through the lens of modern science, we finally have the means to test whether Pythagoras was right”.


⭐⭐ Safron’s Integrated World Modeling Theory


  • The “Integrated World Modeling Theory” (IWMT) combines multiple theories into a unified model of consciousness to account for core aspects of phenomenology across computational/functional, algorithmic, and implementational/mechanistic (supervenient) levels of analysis.
  • Phenomenal consciousness is what it is like to be the spatiotemporally and causally coherent functioning of probabilistic generative model for the (valenced, affectively biased) sensoriums of embodied–embedded agents, entailing either perception or imagination when respectively coupled to or decoupled from sense-data.
  • IWMT is distinct from the theories it attempts to integrate in suggesting that no theory taken in isolation is capable of identifying sufficient conditions for explaining why there should be "something that it feels like" to be a physical/computational system.
  • Further developments in this theory may allow for sufficiently precise specifications of physical/computational substrates of consciousness in biological systems, so that they may be more skillfully understood and intervened upon, and potentially developed by artificial means.
  • IWMT draws upon both Integration Information Theory, Global Neuronal Workspace, and Predictive Processing theories, to describe how subnetworks of the brain can act as shared latent (work)spaces amongst heterogeneous sensory modalities, and arenas for Bayesian model selection, generating system-world estimates with sufficient rapidity to both inform and be informed by action-perception cycles, so affording more flexible adaptive behavior.


⭐⭐ Moll’s Multitrack Consciousness Conjecture


A Multitrack Consciousness Conjecture (MCC) suggests a new structure for consciousness in time, aiming to resolve the paradox of our subjective experience of a privileged now that seems fundamentally at odds with the physical model of block time (also known as the block universe).


  • "The ontology of consciousness within spacetime".
  • Each conscious self exists on its own track in time, experiencing a unique now within the block universe.
  • Explains our sense of now without denying block time; uses its structure to ground temporal experience.
  • These tracks are isolated in spacetime, with no cross-access—each one feels present only in its own moment.
  • Makes eternalist physics and our felt experience of time compatible, resolving the privileged now paradox.


The block universe framework, rooted in Einstein’s relativity, posits a four-dimensional spacetime where all moments—past, present, and future—are equally real within the structure of spacetime. Yet, we as conscious beings experience time as flowing, with a specific privileged now moment that moves inexorably towards the future. This apparent contradiction raises a profound question: if we live in a block time universe, why do we experience the specific present moment as now rather than any other moment?

Unlike the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics, which involves branching realities encompassing alternate versions of reality, the basic version of the multitrack conjecture operates within a single worldline. It frames the experience of the self as a multiplicity of localizations within a fixed spacetime structure. Still, the multitrack conjecture may equally resolve the privileged now paradox in the context of branching time interpretations of quantum mechanics.
